6 real scenarios about the OSAP & tuition crisis in Ontario.
Housing. Textbooks. Debt. Hunger. What's your move?

Scenario 1 of 8
🏠
Housing Crisis
Your rent goes up $400 next month and OSAP doesn't cover it.
You're a first-year student from a low-income family in a city 3 hours from home. Your OSAP covers tuition and a portion of living costs — but your landlord just raised rent. You're $400 short every month and can't find a cheaper unit in the area.
In your head think: Should the government be responsible for closing this gap? What systemic change would fix this?
Scenario 2 of 8
📚
Textbook Costs
Your required textbook costs $320. Your prof says you need it for every class.
It's week one. The course syllabus lists a required textbook: $320 new, $240 used — both sold out at the library reserve. You have $50 left in your account until your next OSAP installment in 6 weeks. The prof has said no late submissions.
In your head think: Is it fair to require $300+ textbooks when OSAP hasn't adjusted for rising costs? Who's responsible?
Scenario 3 of 8
🍜
Food Insecurity
You've been skipping meals to make rent. Your GPA is slipping.
After rent, groceries aren't in the budget. You've been eating once a day. You're exhausted in class and your grades dropped from an A to a C. A friend says the campus food bank is "embarrassing to use." Midterms are in 2 weeks.
In your head think: 1 in 3 Canadian post-secondary students face food insecurity. Why is this not treated as a crisis?
Scenario 4 of 8
💸
OSAP Repayment
You graduated with $45,000 in debt. Your first job pays $38,000/year.
You finished your degree — but you owe $45,000 in OSAP loans. Your entry-level job pays ~$2,600/month after tax. Rent is $1,400. Loan repayment starts in 6 months at $480/month.
In your head think: Should post-secondary be tuition-free like some European countries? What are the trade-offs?
Scenario 5 of 8
🛑
Dropping Out
You're considering dropping out. School costs too much. Work pays enough to survive.
You're halfway through second year, accumulating $20,000 in debt per year. A full-time job offer just landed: $22/hr, no degree required. Your family says "just finish" — but they're not the ones paying.
In your head think: When financial barriers force students to drop out, who is responsible — the individual, institution, or government?
Scenario 6 of 8
✊
Taking Action
You want to fight for change. What's your first move?
You've seen how the OSAP and tuition crisis affects students every day. You're angry and want to do something. You have limited time, no budget, and 400 followers. What's your first step toward real change?
In your head think: Which creates the most sustainable change? Which spreads like a social epidemic — where others can join and keep it going?
Scenario 7 of 8
🧠
Mental Health
You're burning out. Anxiety is affecting your grades and your sleep.
Between working part-time, keeping up with assignments, and stressing about tuition debt, you haven't slept properly in weeks. Your anxiety is at an all-time high and you missed two midterms. Campus counselling has a 6-week waitlist.
In your head think: Is financial stress a mental health issue? Should OSAP and tuition reform include support for student wellbeing?
Scenario 8 of 8
🌍
International Students
Your international classmate pays $32,000/year in tuition. She's not eligible for OSAP.
Your friend came from abroad to study in Ontario. She pays 4x what domestic students pay and gets zero government aid. She works 30 hours a week just to survive — which is hurting her grades. Her visa requires full-time enrollment.
In your head think: Is the tuition crisis only a domestic student issue? How does it connect to equity and systemic barriers for all students?
