6 real scenarios about the OSAP & tuition crisis in Ontario.
Housing. Textbooks. Debt. Hunger. What's your move?

Scenario 1 of 6
🏠
Housing Crisis
Your rent goes up $400 next month and OSAP doesn't cover it.
You're a first-year student from a low-income family in a city 3 hours from home. Your OSAP covers tuition and a portion of living costs — but your landlord just raised rent. You're $400 short every month and can't find a cheaper unit in the area.
In your head think: Should the government be responsible for closing this gap? What systemic change would fix this?

Scenario 2 of 6
📚
Textbook Costs
Your required textbook costs $320. Your prof says you need it for every class.
It's week one. The course syllabus lists a required textbook: $320 new, $240 used — both sold out at the library reserve. You have $50 left in your account until your next OSAP installment in 6 weeks. The prof has said no late submissions.
In your head think: Is it fair to require $300+ textbooks when OSAP hasn't adjusted for rising costs? Who's responsible for fixing this?
2 / 6
Scenario 3 of 6
🍜
Food Insecurity
You've been skipping meals to make rent. Your GPA is slipping.
After rent, groceries aren't in the budget. You've been eating once a day. You're exhausted in class and your grades have dropped from an A to a C. A friend tells you the campus food bank is "embarrassing to use." Midterms are in 2 weeks.
In your head think: 1 in 3 Canadian post-secondary students experience food insecurity. Why is this not treated as a crisis?
3 / 6
Scenario 4 of 6
💸
OSAP Repayment
You graduated with $45,000 in debt. Your first job pays $38,000/year.
Congratulations — you finished your degree! But you owe $45,000 in OSAP loans. Your entry-level job pays $38K/year (~$2,600/month after tax). Rent is $1,400. Loan repayment starts in 6 months. OSAP calculates your monthly payment at $480.
In your head think: Should post-secondary education in Canada be tuition-free like some European countries? What are the trade-offs?
4 / 6
Scenario 5 of 6
🛑
Dropping Out
You're considering dropping out. School costs too much. Work pays enough to survive.
You're halfway through second year. Between tuition hikes, rent, and textbooks, you're accumulating $20,000 in debt per year. A full-time job offer just landed: $22/hr, no degree required. Your family says "just finish" — but they're not the ones paying.
In your head think: When financial barriers force students to drop out, who is responsible — the individual, the institution, or the government?

Scenario 6 of 6
✊
Taking Action
You want to fight for change. What's your first move?
You've seen how the OSAP and tuition crisis affects students like you every day. You're angry and want to do something about it. You have limited time, no budget, and a social media following of 400 people. What's your first step toward creating real change?
In your head think: Which of these creates the most sustainable change? Which connects to a social epidemic — where others can join and spread the cause?
